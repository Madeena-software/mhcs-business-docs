## 1. Name and Origin

**MHCS stands for Madeena Health Care System.**

The name **Madeena** was inspired by **“Made in Ina” — Made in Indonesia**.

MHCS is therefore positioned as an **Indonesia-led healthcare system**. This does not mean every technology must be built in Indonesia. It means the healthcare problem, service design, localisation, governance, implementation, and accountability remain anchored in Indonesian needs.

> **Made in Indonesia, for the People of Indonesia.**

---

## 2. What Is MHCS?

> **Madeena Health Care System (MHCS) is an Indonesia-led healthcare orchestration and coordination system that brings together and localises existing Government, clinical, technological, and partner capabilities into continuous end-to-end healthcare services designed around validated healthcare delivery gaps.**

Its purpose is to help ensure that people receive the care they need and that the intended health outcome is achieved and, where appropriate, maintained over time.

### Short definition

> **MHCS orchestrates existing healthcare capabilities into continuous end-to-end healthcare services.**

### Plain-language definition

> **MHCS helps make sure healthcare does not stop at screening, a result, data entry, or a referral. It helps keep the healthcare journey moving until the required care is delivered and the intended health outcome is reached.**

---

## 3. Why MHCS Exists

Indonesia already has many healthcare capabilities:

- Government systems;
- hospitals and Puskesmas;
- doctors and specialists;
- laboratories;
- imaging systems;
- PACS and clinical systems;
- AI;
- referral mechanisms;
- payers;
- communication channels;
- and technology partners.

The problem is therefore not always that a technology or capability is missing.

The problem may be that these capabilities do not yet work together as **one completed healthcare service**.

For example:

> screening may happen, but follow-up may not;

> a referral may be created, but not completed;

> a result may be produced, but the required clinical action may not happen;

> treatment may start, but long-term control may not be maintained.

MHCS exists to help close this type of **healthcare delivery gap**.

## 4. MHCS as the Conductor

The simplest way to understand MHCS is through an orchestra.

### The healthcare ecosystem is the orchestra

The orchestra may include:

- SATUSEHAT;
- SITB and other Government systems;
- BPJS and payer mechanisms;
- doctors and specialists;
- hospitals and Puskesmas;
- laboratories;
- imaging systems;
- PACS;
- AI;
- communication channels;
- Madeena;
- and external partners.

### The validated healthcare problem is the score

Different healthcare problems require different combinations of capabilities.

TB may require one composition.

Primary-care imaging may require another.

Hypertension, cancer, maternal health, or another healthcare problem may require another.

### MHCS is the conductor

> **MHCS does not need to own or build every capability.**

Its role is to coordinate the right capabilities around the validated healthcare problem so that they work together as one service.

The value of MHCS is therefore not simply that it can connect technologies.

> **The value of MHCS is that it helps coordinate the right capabilities so the healthcare pathway continues to its intended outcome.**

---

## 5. The Four Core Functions

The core MHCS logic is:

> **Orchestration → Integration → Localisation → Delivery**

### Orchestration

Orchestration is the central role of MHCS.

It coordinates:

- what needs to happen;
- who or what should act next;
- when the next action should happen;
- whether the required action has actually happened;
- what remains incomplete;
- and whether the healthcare pathway has reached its intended outcome.

### Integration

Integration allows the required systems and capabilities to exchange the information needed to work together.

> **Integration connects capabilities. Orchestration coordinates what those capabilities must collectively accomplish.**

### Localisation

Localisation adapts the service to Indonesia.

This can include:

- Government systems;
- clinical workflow;
- regulation;
- payer mechanisms;
- infrastructure;
- connectivity;
- workforce realities;
- language;
- digital literacy;
- and the communication channels actually used by people.

Localisation is therefore much more than translating an interface.

### Delivery

Delivery means the healthcare service actually happens.

A pathway is not complete merely because:

- an AI result exists;
- an image was read;
- an API succeeded;
- data entered SATUSEHAT;
- or a referral was created.

> **Delivery continues until the required healthcare action is completed.**

## 6. Continuous End-to-End Healthcare

MHCS is intended to support **continuous end-to-end healthcare**.

**End-to-end** means the relevant healthcare pathway continues from entry to the intended clinical or service outcome.

For example:

```text
Screening / Detection
        ↓
Assessment / Diagnosis
        ↓
Required Clinical Action
        ↓
Treatment / Referral
        ↓
Follow-up
        ↓
Intended Health Outcome
```

But healthcare does not always end after one episode.

A person's health changes over time.

```text
Healthy
   ↓
Prevention / Monitoring
   ↓
Screening
   ↓
Risk or Finding?
   ├── No → Remain Healthy → Continue Monitoring ↺
   │
   └── Yes
        ↓
     Diagnosis
        ↓
     Treatment
        ↓
     Follow-up
        ↓
 Controlled / Recovered
        ↓
 Continued Monitoring ↺
```

Therefore:

> **MHCS does not stop when an immediate endpoint is reached if the health outcome requires continued monitoring or control.**

The final question is not simply:

> **“Did the technology work?”**

It is:

> **“Did the person receive the care they needed, and was the intended health outcome reached and maintained where necessary?”**

## 7. Relationship with SATUSEHAT and Government Systems

MHCS does **not** replace SATUSEHAT, SITB, BPJS, or other authoritative Government systems.

It also should not be positioned as controlling them.

> **Government systems remain authoritative within their respective domains.**

SATUSEHAT can be understood as an authoritative national interoperability and health-data backbone.

MHCS works **with** SATUSEHAT and other Government systems as part of the healthcare pathway.

Conceptually:

```text
Government Systems      Clinical Capabilities
        \                     /
         \                   /
          ┌─────────────────┐
          │      MHCS       │
          │ Orchestration & │
          │  Coordination   │
          └─────────────────┘
         /         |         \
        /          |          \
 Technology      Partners     Delivery Channels
```

Relevant data should reach the appropriate authoritative Government system where required.

But:

> **data being recorded is not necessarily the same as healthcare being completed.**

## 8. Channels Are Part of Delivery

MHCS is not defined by one application.

A healthcare service may use different channels depending on the population and operating environment, such as:

- SATUSEHAT Mobile;
- web;
- WhatsApp;
- SMS;
- email;
- telephone;
- assisted access;
- fasyankes systems;
- or other appropriate channels.

The principle is:

> **Channel follows the people, not the other way around.**

A person may benefit from an MHCS-orchestrated service without ever opening a dedicated MHCS application.

The channel must still comply with appropriate privacy, consent, authentication, and health-data governance requirements.

---

## 9. MHCS Does Not Need to Own Everything

MHCS should follow a reuse-first principle:

> **Use existing capability → integrate → localise → partner → build only where genuinely missing.**

MHCS does not inherently need to own:

- SATUSEHAT;
- SITB;
- every RME or HIS;
- every PACS;
- every AI model;
- every imaging device;
- every cloud platform;
- every laboratory;
- every communication channel;
- or every healthcare professional network.

> **Ownership is not the objective. Effective orchestration and delivery are.**

---

## 10. What MHCS Is Not

MHCS should not be positioned as:

- a replacement for SATUSEHAT;
- a replacement for Government programme systems;
- a generic RME/HIS;
- a PACS;
- a generic teleradiology platform;
- an AI doctor;
- a national super-app;
- a collection of unrelated software features;
- or a system whose value is merely connecting APIs.

MHCS is also not justified simply because technology exists.

> **The healthcare delivery gap must come first.**
